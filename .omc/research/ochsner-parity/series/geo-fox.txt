 Frequently Asked Questions about the OCHSNER GEO FOX
 Which heat sources can the GEO FOX tap into?
The heat source ground can be accessed either through flat collectors or ground probes. Flat collectors are laid horizontally in the ground and are subject to notification. Ground probes go vertically into the depth – a permit from the lower water authority is required from 100 m. Which variant is sensible depends on the size of the property, soil conditions, and local circumstances.
 Is a heating buffer storage necessary?
No. Thanks to inverter technology, the GEO FOX continuously adapts to the current heat demand – an additional buffer storage is not required. This saves space, installation effort, and costs.
 Can the GEO FOX also cool?
Yes. With optional additional equipment, passive cooling is possible. Heat is conducted from the surface heating through the ground probes to the ground. Since the ground is significantly cooler at around 10 °C in summer than the room air, indoor temperatures can be noticeably lowered – with minimal electricity consumption, as only the circulation pump runs.
 What does R290 as a refrigerant mean?
R290 is propane – a natural refrigerant with very low greenhouse potential. It operates thermodynamically highly efficiently and is not synthetically produced. The GEO FOX exclusively uses R290.
 Tower S200 or indoor unit S1 – what is the difference?
The Tower S200 combines heat pump and hot water storage in a compact device with less than 0.5 m² of floor space. The indoor unit S1 does not have its own storage – it is used where hot water preparation is done through a separate storage. Which version fits depends on the available space and the planned hydraulics.
